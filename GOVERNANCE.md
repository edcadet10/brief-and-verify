# Governance

Brief & Verify uses lightweight maintainer governance suited to a small public project.

## Roles

- **Contributors** propose issues, discussions, pull requests, reviews, research, and teaching adaptations.
- **Maintainers** set project scope, review contributions, manage releases and community spaces, and make final merge decisions.

Repeated, constructive contribution may lead to broader review or maintenance responsibility as the project grows. Access is granted deliberately and can be limited to the area a contributor knows well.

## How decisions are made

Routine, reversible changes are decided through pull-request review. Changes to curriculum sequence, measurement claims, public data behavior, dependencies, or adapter contracts should begin with an issue or Discussion.

Maintainers weigh:

1. usefulness for the project’s public audiences;
2. accuracy and strength of evidence;
3. accessibility, privacy, and learner safety;
4. model neutrality and deterministic offline usefulness;
5. fit with the static-first architecture; and
6. long-term maintenance cost.

Consensus is preferred. When consensus is not practical, the repository maintainer makes the final call and explains material tradeoffs in the issue or pull request.

## Roadmap and releases

Open issues and Discussions are the public roadmap. Labels identify scope and approachable work; a label is not a delivery promise.

Releases follow semantic versioning where practical and are summarized in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md). Security fixes may be prepared privately before public disclosure.

## Changing this document

Governance changes use the same public pull-request process. A proposal should explain the problem, who is affected, and how the change improves transparent participation.
